Golden Ruler: a calibration ruler for numeric formats

2026-11-27 · 6 min read

[software vectors, not hardware measurements; the ml_dtypes cross-validation is as reported in the paper] Golden Ruler (arXiv:2606.09686) is a catalogue of 109 numeric formats in 13 clusters and six bit-exact conformance packs: GF16, MXFP4 element, BF16, FP8 E4M3, FP8 E5M2 and E8M0 block scale. Each pack is a self-contained JSON document with a SHA-256 fingerprint, a single row schema and an anchor vector that encodes 3.0 through the identity phi^2 + 1/phi^2 = 3 as a cross-pack sanity check. #NumberFormats#Verification#GF16#OpenSource

Every new AI-inference chip brings its own numeric format: FP8 with two exponent variants, BF16, MXFP4, block-scale formats — and dozens of research variants. An engineer porting a model between accelerators meets silent divergences that are hard to diagnose without a shared reference ruler.

What we published

Golden Ruler (arXiv:2606.09686) is a catalogue of 109 numeric formats in 13 clusters and six bit-exact conformance packs: GF16, MXFP4 element, BF16, FP8 E4M3, FP8 E5M2 and E8M0 block scale. Each pack is a self-contained JSON document with a SHA-256 fingerprint, a single row schema and an anchor vector that encodes 3.0 through the identity phi^2 + 1/phi^2 = 3 — as a cross-pack sanity check.

Cross-validation against ml_dtypes 0.5.4 (Google/JAX), as reported in the paper: element-level rounding of the float formats (BF16, FP8 E4M3/E5M2, GF16) was compared; the block-scale formats (E8M0, MXFP4 element) were checked separately, their divergences documented explicitly and interpreted as a permitted interpretation gap of the specification, not hidden.

Why it matters

  • Porting a model between accelerators stops being a lottery: a divergence is localised down to the format and the vector.
  • QA teams get ready golden vectors instead of building tests by hand.
  • A publication with fingerprints is reproducible by anyone, without taking it on trust.

Honest limitations

  • This is a catalogue and a methodology, not a superiority claim about any format.
  • The IEEE P3109 cross-walk is version v3.2.0; the standard is still moving.
  • No hardware measurements were made for Golden Ruler — these are software vectors.

Artifacts

All packs and tools are open: github.com/gHashTag/t27 (pack availability and status in the repo; for the float formats the evidence is complete, for the block-scale formats it carries documented caveats). Full text: arxiv.org/abs/2606.09686.
